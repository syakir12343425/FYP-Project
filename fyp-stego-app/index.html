    1. Executive Summary

    This report documents a simulation conducted in the steganographic verification workspace. We benchmarked spatial Least Significant Bit (LSB) embedding against a convolutional neural network (CNN) Encoder-Decoder model subject to image downscaling processing (attacks). The evaluations demonstrate the high fragility of classic spatial encoding and verify the robustness of deep representation training.

    3. Methodological Discussion

    LSB steganography writes each message bit into the least significant bit of a pixel value. Downscaling resamples and averages neighbouring pixels, so those exact bits do not survive, and extraction is no better than guessing (about 45% BER). The CNN encoder adds a small learned residual (at most about 3% of the pixel range) to the cover image. During training, a differentiable downscaling layer (nearest, bilinear, bicubic and area, to 224–384 px, with 8-bit quantisation) sat between encoder and decoder, so both networks learned a representation that survives resizing. Lanczos was held out of training and still decodes at a similar error rate. The first trained model hid the message mostly in colour, which left a visible tint (about 32 dB PSNR); the model used here was fine-tuned with an extra penalty on colour change and with WhatsApp-style attacks (JPEG, WebP and no resize) added to training. It reaches about 35.5 dB PSNR, versus about 80 dB for LSB; a faint colour pattern can still be seen in large white areas when zoomed in. Each message is protected with Reed–Solomon error correction (RS(128,64) over GF(28): 63 message bytes plus 64 parity bytes, repairing up to 32 damaged bytes) and then whitened with a fixed public keystream; whitening is not encryption. The bit error rate reported here is the raw rate before error correction.
