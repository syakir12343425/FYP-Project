3. Methodological Discussion

LSB steganography writes each message bit into the least significant bit of a pixel value. Downscaling resamples and averages neighbouring pixels, so those exact bits do not survive, and extraction is no better than guessing (about 48% BER). The CNN encoder adds a small learned residual (at most about 3% of the pixel range) to the cover image. During training, a differentiable downscaling layer (nearest, bilinear, bicubic and area, to 224–384 px, with 8-bit quantisation) sat between encoder and decoder, so both networks learned a representation that survives resizing. Lanczos was held out of training and still decodes at a similar error rate. The trade-off is image quality: about 32 dB PSNR for the CNN versus about 80 dB for LSB. Messages are whitened with a fixed public keystream before embedding; this is not encryption.
